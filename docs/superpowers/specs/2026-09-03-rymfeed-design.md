# `-rymfeed` — RYM activity feed to Discord

**Status:** design complete, **blocked on transport** — see [What remains](#what-remains)
**Date:** 2026-09-03
**Target version:** 2.12.0 (new command family + new tables → minor)

## Goal

A server admin points the bot at a channel. Members who are logged into RYM with the bot opt
themselves in. The bot polls each opted-in member's RYM activity feed and posts an embed when they
rate something new.

Members opt in and out for themselves. Channel setup, interval, and switching the whole thing off
require Manage Server.

## What the RYM feed actually is

Measured on 2026-09-03 against `https://rateyourmusic.com/~mattbennett/data/rss`, viewed in a
browser (see [Transport](#transport-no-viable-path-today) for why not by script).

The feed is real, current, and public — a logged-out incognito window reaches it after clearing the
Cloudflare challenge. It is also served carelessly, and four of its defects drive the design.

### It is a rolling window, not a history

35 items covering 2026-08-15 → 2026-09-03. Whether the cap is by count or by age is unknown and
does not matter: the feed forgets. Two consequences.

- Polling can never backfill. Anything older than the window is unreachable.
- **A newly enabled member must be seeded silently.** Their first successful fetch records a
  watermark and posts nothing, or enabling one person dumps three weeks of ratings into the channel.

### There is no `<guid>`

RSS 2.0's stable-identity element is absent from every item. Dedupe has to be synthesized, and the
obvious synthesis is wrong: keying on `<link>` alone would permanently suppress a **re-rate**, which
emits a fresh item with the same link and a new `pubDate`.

We key on `pubDate` instead — see [Dedupe](#dedupe-a-watermark-not-a-seen-table).

### The artist field is wrong in 85% of items

Across the 34 rating items in the sample: 27 say `Multiple Artists`, 2 say `Various Artists`, and 5
name the real artist. RYM is leaking its internal artist-credit model into the title, and it is wrong
even for solo records.

```
Rated An Insatiable High by Multiple Artists 3.0 stars
  → /release/album/高中正義/an-insatiable-high/     ← Masayoshi Takanaka

Rated Arthur Verocai by Various Artists 3.0 stars
  → /release/album/arthur-verocai/…                ← Arthur Verocai, a solo record

Rated Playing It Cool & Playing It Right by Various Artists 3.5 stars
  → /release/album/keith-hudson/…                  ← Keith Hudson
```

The `<link>` path holds the truth. Prefer it over the title: URLs are identifiers RYM must keep
stable to avoid breaking every inbound link on the internet, while titles are prose it regenerates
from a lossy model.

### It is "recent activity", not "recent ratings"

The sample contains a list (`/list/mattbennett/crackles-clicks-comfort/`) with no `<description>` and
no `Rated … stars` shape. Reviews and other activity types are presumably possible too. Filtering by
item shape is mandatory.

`<description>` itself is dead weight: every rating item carries the identical body, a link to
`/collection/<user>/recent`. All embed richness is generated by us, not received.

### Standards compliance, for the record

| Defect | Effect |
|---|---|
| No `<guid>` on items | Dedupe must be synthesized |
| `<channel>` missing required `<link>` and `<description>` | Feed is invalid RSS 2.0 |
| `content-type: application/xhtml+xml; charset=utf-8` | Wrong for a feed; strict readers may reject |

Lenient parsers handle all three by sniffing the body; strict ones may reject the feed outright. The
parser choice in [Parsing](#parsing) accounts for this.

## Transport: no viable path today

Every non-browser client we tried gets `403` with `cf-mitigated: challenge`. Cloudflare's managed
challenge fingerprints the TLS handshake and HTTP/2 settings, below anything request headers can
reach:

```
curl, no UA                      → 403 challenge
curl, Chrome 140 UA              → 403 challenge
curl, full browser header set    → 403 challenge
node fetch (undici), browser UA  → 403 challenge
https://rateyourmusic.com/ root  → 403 challenge
```

`robots.txt` line 1 is not boilerplate:

> `# Sonemic, Inc. prohibits any kind of automated means, (e.g. crawling, scraping, etc) of access to the service without express permission.`

**We are not building challenge evasion.** No TLS-fingerprint impersonation, no stealth headless
browser, no `cf_clearance` harvesting, no proxy rotation. That is engineering around an access
control on a site that prohibits automated access without permission, and it is also the brittle
choice — Cloudflare rotates its challenge and the feature dies silently on a Tuesday.

The design therefore keeps the fetch behind a one-method interface so the rest of it stays valid
whatever the transport turns out to be. But every candidate transport we can reach today is either
blocked or against RYM's stated policy, which is what the rest of this section establishes.

### Ruled out: every relay, on policy as well as mechanism

The original plan was to read from a relay the operator runs — a self-hosted reader, or a hosted one
like Feedly or Inoreader. **Both are ruled out.** Measured 2026-09-03.

A self-hosted reader on the feed1 VM is just another Go/PHP HTTP client: its own TLS fingerprint, no
challenge solving, and an Oracle Cloud datacenter IP with mediocre reputation. Self-hosting
relocates the request without changing what Cloudflare thinks of it. Noted because it is the
intuitive answer and it does not work.

A hosted reader fails too. Three independent third-party servers, all long-established, all blocked:

```
W3C Feed Validator  → Server returned HTTP Error 403: Forbidden
rss2json            → "Cannot download this RSS feed"
allorigins          → 522
```

And `robots.txt` — 302 lines, ~60 named agents, **53 of them a blanket `Disallow: /`** — ends with:

```
User-agent: *
Disallow: /
```

Deny by default. The only carve-outs with a real allow-list are search and social-preview bots
(`googlebot`, `Googlebot-Image`, `msnbot`, `Slurp`, `Twitterbot`, `Mediapartners-Google`). **No feed
reader is permitted** — `AwarioRssBot`, `NewsNow`, `Jetslide`, and `news-please` are all in the
blocked cluster.

So even a reader that *could* fetch the feed would be acting against RYM's stated policy. Feedly
succeeding would mean Feedly is in Cloudflare's verified-bot list while ignoring RYM's robots.txt;
that is not a foundation to build a feature on.

### What remains

1. **Express permission from Sonemic.** The one path `robots.txt` itself names. If granted, the
   design below ships unchanged with a `DirectRssSource` doing one polite conditional GET per user
   per interval, identifying itself honestly. Uncertain timeline, may get no reply.
2. **Self-reported ratings.** Members post via a command; the bot owns the channel routing, opt-in,
   and embeds. Everything downstream of the source seam survives; the parser, the watermark, the
   scheduler, and roughly half this spec do not. That is a *different feature* wearing this one's
   interface, and it needs its own spec rather than an edit to this one.
3. **Shelve.** The design is recorded and the constraints are measured, so picking it up later costs
   nothing but reading this file.

Until one is chosen, this spec is **blocked, not cancelled**. Everything from
[Architecture](#architecture) down is transport-independent and stays correct under option 1.

## Architecture

Six modules. The split follows the repo's existing conventions: a pure parser and a pure formatter
sit in sibling modules so they can be unit tested without Discord or HTTP, exactly as `fm.ts` and
`fmFormat.ts` do.

```
src/rym/
  feedParse.ts     pure: XML string → RymActivityItem[]; slug → artist name
  source.ts        RymFeedSource interface + ReaderApiSource + FakeSource
  feedService.ts   config, opt-in, watermark, orchestration (the only DB writer)
  feedFormat.ts    pure: RatingItem[] → EmbedBuilder
  worker.ts        RymFeedScheduler — deadline-driven poller
src/commands/
  rymFeed.ts       the -rymfeed command
```

`src/rym/` is a new subsystem directory alongside `crowns/` and `banner/`. The existing
`src/commands/rym.ts` stays where it is and is untouched — those 22 commands are pure URL builders
and share nothing with this.

### The transport seam

```ts
export interface RymFeedSource {
  fetch(rymUsername: string, sourceRef: string | null): Promise<FeedFetchResult>;
}

export type FeedFetchResult =
  | { ok: true; items: RymActivityItem[] }
  | { ok: false; reason: 'blocked' | 'not-found' | 'unparseable' | 'network'; detail: string };
```

Everything else in the design is written against this interface, which is why the unsettled
transport does not block implementation. `FakeSource` serves the whole test suite; the real adapter
is a leaf.

`blocked` is a distinct reason from `network` because it means something different operationally —
the relay is being challenged, and no amount of retrying will help.

### Parsing

`RymActivityItem` is whatever the feed emitted; `RatingItem` is a parsed rating.

```ts
interface RymActivityItem { title: string; link: string; pubDate: Date }

interface RatingItem {
  albumTitle: string;    // from the title, between "Rated " and " by "
  artistSlug: string;    // from the link path
  artistName: string;    // de-slugged, later overwritten by Last.fm if enrichment succeeds
  stars: number;         // 0.5–5.0
  link: string;
  ratedAt: Date;
}
```

Items are classified by shape. Anything not matching `Rated <album> by <artist> <score> stars` is
discarded — lists, reviews, and any activity type RYM adds later. Discarded items still advance the
watermark, so they can never resurface if the filter widens.

The artist comes from the link path, never the title. De-slugging is best-effort and documented as
lossy: `daryl-hall_john-oates` → `Daryl Hall / John Oates`, `miguel_a__ruiz` → `Miguel A. Ruiz`,
and unicode slugs like `高中正義` pass through unchanged. When de-slugging is uncertain the slug
itself is the fallback — Last.fm enrichment is what makes this good, and de-slugging only has to be
good enough to feed it.

**XML parser:** `fast-xml-parser`, a new dependency. Hand-rolled regex over XML is fragile against
CDATA, entities, and the unicode already present in these slugs.

The raw feed *is* well-formed with respect to entities — confirmed 2026-09-03 against View Source,
where the ampersand appears correctly escaped as `Robson Jorge &amp; Lincoln Olivetti`. The bare `&`
visible in a browser's XML tree is a rendering artifact. So the parser needs leniency for sloppy
*metadata* (missing `<guid>`, missing required `<channel>` children, wrong content-type), not
recovery from malformed XML.

**Whitespace is not single-spaced.** Raw source shows `by Multiple Artists  3.0 stars` — two spaces
before the score. Every pattern in the parser uses `\s+`, never a literal space. A regex written
against the browser-rendered text would silently match nothing.

### Enrichment

For each new rating, `LastfmClient.getAlbumInfo(artistName, albumTitle)` supplies the canonical
artist name and album art via the existing `highestResImageUrl` helper, so these embeds match the
rest of the bot.

- A failure or miss is swallowed and the embed renders with the de-slugged name and no art. This
  follows the repo's rule that edits which only *add* optional detail must not discard an
  already-good message.
- Cost is one call per **new** rating through the shared 250 ms limiter — a few dozen a week at
  observed volume. Negligible, and it does not page.

### Embeds

Two shapes, chosen by count:

- **Exactly one new rating** → a rich single-album embed: album title hyperlinked to the release,
  artist, stars, album art as the thumbnail. This is the common case and it should look good.
- **Two or more** → one digest embed per member per poll, titled for the member, each rating a
  description line (`[Album](link) — ★ 3.5`), built with `paginateLines` from `src/core/paginate.ts`.
  The newest album's art becomes the thumbnail.

One embed per member per poll in both cases, so burst size never affects Discord rate limits.
Observed bursts justify this: six ratings on Aug 21, four on Aug 25 with three inside 90 minutes.

Star rendering and exact footer wording are for implementation. This is a new command, so nothing
here is bound by the legacy-output-fidelity rule that governs the ported commands.

## Schema

Two tables. Migration generated with `npm run db:generate -- --name add_rym_feed_tables`.

```ts
export const rymFeedConfigs = sqliteTable(
  'rym_feed_configs',
  {
    guildId: text('guild_id').primaryKey(),
    channelId: text('channel_id').notNull(),
    intervalMinutes: integer('interval_minutes').notNull(),
    nextRunAt: integer('next_run_at', { mode: 'timestamp_ms' }).notNull(),
    lastPolledAt: integer('last_polled_at', { mode: 'timestamp_ms' }),
    failureCount: integer('failure_count').notNull().default(0),
    lastError: text('last_error'),
    setBy: text('set_by').notNull(),
    createdAt: createdAt(),
  },
  (t) => [index('rym_feed_configs_next_run').on(t.nextRunAt)],
);

export const rymFeedUsers = sqliteTable(
  'rym_feed_users',
  {
    id: integer('id').primaryKey({ autoIncrement: true }),
    guildId: text('guild_id').notNull(),
    discordUserId: text('discord_user_id').notNull(),
    enabled: integer('enabled', { mode: 'boolean' }).notNull().default(true),
    /** transport-specific handle, e.g. an Inoreader stream id; null = derive from rym_username */
    sourceRef: text('source_ref'),
    /** newest pubDate already accounted for; null = not yet seeded, so the next fetch posts nothing */
    watermarkAt: integer('watermark_at', { mode: 'timestamp_ms' }),
    createdAt: createdAt(),
  },
  (t) => [uniqueIndex('rym_feed_users_guild_user').on(t.guildId, t.discordUserId)],
);
```

`rym_feed_configs` deliberately mirrors `banner_configs` — same deadline column, same failure
counting, same `setBy`. A reader who knows one knows the other.

`sourceRef` is the schema half of the transport seam. Under a direct-feed transport it stays null and
the URL derives from `users.rym_username`; under a reader API it holds that reader's handle for the
subscription.

### Dedupe: a watermark, not a seen table

`watermarkAt` holds the newest `pubDate` already accounted for. A poll posts items with
`pubDate > watermarkAt`, then sets the watermark to the maximum `pubDate` in the fetch.

This is exact for this feed, and worth spelling out why, because a watermark is usually a
lossy shortcut:

- A re-rate emits a new item with a later `pubDate`, so it posts correctly. Keying on `<link>` would
  have suppressed it forever.
- Two items sharing one `pubDate` arrive in the same fetch, so both post and the watermark lands on
  their shared value. The only loss case is a *future* item at that exact same second, which does
  not happen in practice.

The alternative — a `rym_feed_seen` table of item keys — is exact in that edge case too, at the cost
of a third table that grows without bound and needs a pruning job. Not worth it for an edge case
that requires two ratings in the same second across two different polls.

## Commands

`-rymfeed <subcommand>`, following the `-banner` split between open and Manage-Server subcommands.

| Subcommand | Perm | Behavior |
|---|---|---|
| `-rymfeed here` | Manage Server | Set this channel as the output; create or update the guild config |
| `-rymfeed interval <minutes>` | Manage Server | Clamped to 30–1440, default 60 |
| `-rymfeed stop` | Manage Server | Delete the guild config. Per-user opt-ins survive, so restarting does not make everyone re-enable |
| `-rymfeed check` | Manage Server | Poll now, ignoring the deadline — the `-banner next` equivalent |
| `-rymfeed on` | — | Opt yourself in. Requires `users.rym_username`; errors with the standard `not logged into rym` message otherwise |
| `-rymfeed off` | — | Opt yourself out. Keeps the row and the watermark, so re-enabling does not replay |
| `-rymfeed status` | — | Channel, interval, next run, opted-in member count, and the last error if any |

Opting in is deliberately *not* gated on Manage Server: it publishes your own listening activity, so
it is your consent to give. Manage Server governs where the bot may post and whether the feature runs
at all.

Registered as `rymFeedCommands` from `src/commands/rymFeed.ts`, wired into `src/index.ts` alongside
`rymCommands`.

## The poller

`RymFeedScheduler` copies `BannerScheduler`: a 60 s tick, `dueConfigs()` by `next_run_at`, a gap
between units of work. Deadline-driven rather than queued, so a missed tick means polling late rather
than losing work — no claim flag, no stuck-job recovery. Catch-up is non-accumulating: a guild that
was due 200 times during an outage polls **once** and reschedules from now.

Two departures from the banner worker, both forced by this feature's shape:

**Fetch is per RYM username, not per guild-member.** One tick collects every distinct
`rym_username` across all enabled members in all due guilds, fetches each **once**, and fans the
result out to every guild that wants it. A member in two servers costs one request, not two. This is
the whole reason the fetch and the posting/watermark logic are separate concerns.

**A failure does not delete the config.** `BannerScheduler` deletes after
`MAX_CONSECUTIVE_FAILURES` because its failure case is a permanently lost boost level. Here the
dominant failure is a transient challenge, so instead: exponential backoff on `next_run_at`, capped
at 24 h, with the reason recorded in `last_error` and surfaced by `-rymfeed status`. A guild is never
silently forgotten and never abandoned — a relay that comes back is picked up within a day, and
`failure_count` resetting on the first success restores the normal interval.

`next_run_at` advances after a failed poll as well as a successful one, or a broken guild would be
retried every tick forever.

### Politeness

A dedicated `RateLimiter` for the feed source, separate from the Last.fm one — different host,
different budget, and the Last.fm limiter's 250 ms is far too aggressive here. Minimum 5 s between
feed requests, plus a per-user gap inside a tick.

## Errors

| Condition | Behavior |
|---|---|
| `blocked` | Backoff, record in `last_error`, surface in `status`. Never retried tightly — retrying a challenge does not solve it |
| `unparseable` | Backoff and report through `app.errors`; this means RYM changed the format and someone should look |
| `not-found` | The member's RYM username is wrong or their account is gone. Report once in `status`; do not disable them automatically, since a rename is theirs to fix |
| Channel deleted or unwritable | Delete the guild config, matching how `BannerScheduler` handles a vanished guild |
| Enrichment failure | Swallowed. The embed still posts |

## Testing

`vitest`, `nock` with net access disabled, and the fakes in `test/helpers/fake.ts` — the existing
setup, no new infrastructure.

- **`feedParse`** — the real 2026-09-03 sample committed as a fixture, since it is the only ground
  truth we have. Asserts: 34 ratings and 1 list found; the list is discarded; artist comes from the
  link for all 29 `Multiple`/`Various Artists` items; half-star scores parse; unicode slugs survive;
  malformed XML fails cleanly rather than throwing.
- **`feedFormat`** — single-rating embed vs digest embed; the count boundary at 1 → 2; enrichment
  present and absent.
- **`feedService`** — seeding posts nothing and sets the watermark; a second poll with no new items
  posts nothing; a re-rate does post; a member in two guilds triggers one fetch and two posts;
  opting off then on does not replay.
- **`worker`** — due selection, non-accumulating catch-up, backoff on failure, `next_run_at`
  advancing after a failure.
- **`rymFeed` command** — permission gating on each subcommand, opt-in without a `rym_username`.

Per CLAUDE.md this is a side project, so breadth is scaled to risk rather than blanket TDD. The
parser and the dedupe logic get real coverage because they are where silent wrongness lives; the
command surface gets enough to prove the permission gates.

## Non-goals

- Any attempt to defeat the Cloudflare challenge.
- Backfilling ratings older than the feed window. Not possible; the feed forgets.
- Posting lists, reviews, or other activity types. The parser discards them and the watermark
  swallows them, so widening later is additive.
- Storing rating history for querying. This feature announces; it does not accumulate. A
  `-rymrecent` built on stored ratings would be a separate spec.
- Guild-configurable embed styling.

## Open questions

Both feed-format spikes are closed: entities are properly escaped in the raw source, and the score is
preceded by multiple spaces. One question remains, and it is a decision rather than an investigation.

1. **Which of the three paths in [What remains](#what-remains)?** Express permission, self-reported
   ratings, or shelve. Nothing else in this spec can proceed until this is answered — it decides
   whether the design ships as written, gets replaced, or waits.
2. **Should `-rymfeed on` require the admin to have run `-rymfeed here` first?** As written, no — you
   may opt in before a channel exists and you start being posted when one does. The alternative
   errors until setup, which is clearer but forces two people to coordinate in order. Only relevant
   under option 1.
